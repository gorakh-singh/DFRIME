Voice is not the wrapper here. It is the product.

The user is an operations team running outbound automated calls: order confirmations, appointment reminders, delivery updates. Every call carries variable data pulled from a database, and the data is the reason for the call.

A customer who cannot make out the phone number does not scroll back and re-read it. There is nothing to re-read. They hang up and call a human, which is the cost the automation was bought to avoid, or they write down something wrong and act on it, which is worse. Take the speech away and there is no product left, only a row in a table that nobody reads.

So the failure mode that matters is not a robotic voice. It is a confident voice saying the wrong characters.

The claim, and what is held constant

Everything below exists to let you falsify one sentence.

Rewriting a string for the ear, through spacing, punctuation and phrasing, measurably improves Rime’s pronunciation of names, phone numbers, addresses and reference codes compared to sending the raw string unchanged, with the model, voice and language held constant.

Where the key lives

The Rime key is read from .env into the Node process. The browser posts to /api/render, which holds the key and calls Rime. No key is ever sent to this page.

What the listening log says

No score is computed from the audio. A machine grading its own pronunciation would prove nothing. These are the verdicts recorded by whoever last sat and listened, including the ones that went against the claim.
